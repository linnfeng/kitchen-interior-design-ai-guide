# Kitchen remodel planning notes

Field notes on comparing kitchen layouts and finishes with AI concept images, kept by the team behind [Kitchen Interior Design](https://kitcheninteriordesignai.online/).

## Start from the room you actually have

Photograph the kitchen from the doorway with the far wall, the window and the appliance positions in frame. Do not tidy the room into submission first: the clutter shows where circulation fails. A concept built on an honest photograph answers a real question, while a concept built on an empty catalogue image only looks decorative.

## One question per round

Generate two or three layout concepts, each answering a single question: would an island fit if the dishwasher moved, what happens if the wall oven is removed, how does the room read with upper cabinets that stop at the window. One question per round keeps the outputs comparable, and comparable outputs are what make a decision possible.

## Check the expensive decisions first

Cabinet finishes, hardware, splashbacks and lighting can change later without opening a wall. Wall position, plumbing, gas lines, window openings and appliance footprints cannot. Spend the concept rounds on the second list, because that is where the money lives.

## Measure before you fall in love

Concept images are generous with space. Measure the wall-to-wall distance and the door swing in the real room, then check each concept against those numbers. An island that leaves a gap too narrow to walk behind while a drawer is open is not a layout, it is a rendering.

## Compare finishes in daylight

Warm evening light flatters almost everything. Run the same comparison in the morning with the blinds open: gloss against matte, warm wood against cool grey, pale worktop against stone effect. North-facing rooms usually want warmer surfaces than south-facing ones.

## Budget from a quotation, not from a picture

A concept sets the ambition; a quotation sets the invoice. Take the image you prefer to two or three suppliers, get real prices for the same specification, and then decide what to change if the numbers are too high.

## Review the boring details

Bin pull-out, microwave location, sockets at the worktop, pantry depth, extractor route and under-cabinet lighting all matter more than the render suggests. Fix them while the concept is still a picture.

## Take it to somebody who builds kitchens

Show the concept to a designer or installer and ask about wall strength, plumbing access, ventilation and delivery times. Twenty minutes of that conversation is cheaper than a redesign.
